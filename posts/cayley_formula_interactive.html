Interactive proof

Cayley’s Formula

How many different trees can be built from n labeled vertices? The answer becomes intuitive once every tree is represented by a Prüfer sequence.

Tn = nn−2

1. Encode with a sequence

Use a sequence (a₁,…,aₙ₋₂), where every entry is one of the labels {1,…,n}.

There are n choices for each of n−2 positions, hence nn−2 sequences.

2. Show a bijection

Counting sequences only works if each sequence gives one tree and each tree gives one sequence.

Prüfer sequences ↔ labeled trees

Interactive decoder

Choose n and a Prüfer sequence of length n−2. Press Build / Reset, then step through the construction.

Why can every sequence be decoded?

Lemma 1 — a leaf candidate always exists

If k vertices remain, the remaining Prüfer sequence has length k−2. It can therefore contain at most k−2 distinct labels. At least two of the k remaining vertices do not appear in it.

So we can always choose the smallest remaining vertex absent from the sequence and attach it to the sequence’s first element.

Lemma 2 — the construction is a tree

At each step we attach a vertex by one edge and then remove that vertex from further consideration. That attachment cannot create a cycle. After n−2 such steps, two vertices remain; connect them.

The result has n vertices, n−1 edges, and no cycle, so it is a tree.

Why are two different sequences different trees?

Lemma 3 — the sequence can be recovered uniquely

Given the finished tree, repeatedly find the smallest leaf, record its unique neighbor, and remove the leaf. Stop when two vertices remain.

This procedure is deterministic, so a tree produces exactly one Prüfer sequence.

Therefore, if sequences A=(a₁,…,aₙ₋₂) and B=(b₁,…,bₙ₋₂) differ at any position, they cannot construct the same tree:

A ≠ B  ⇒  TA ≠ TB
